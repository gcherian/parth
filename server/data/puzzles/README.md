# The Pascal Puzzles — Expanding the Sphere of Knowledge

> *"Knowledge is like a sphere: the more you know, the larger the surface that touches the unknown."*
> — Blaise Pascal

This collection presents **100 puzzles** — one for each philosopher and scientist who expanded the sphere of human understanding. Each puzzle is not designed to test what you know. It is designed to show you the edge where the known meets the unknown — the thrilling boundary where every great mind lived.

---

## How to Use These Puzzles

Each thinker entry contains three challenges graded by depth:

| Level | Ages | Type of Challenge |
|-------|------|-------------------|
| **Beginner** | 6–10 | A hands-on experiment, observation, or first encounter |
| **Intermediate** | 10–14 | A deeper analysis, cross-domain connection, or debate |
| **Advanced** | 14–16+ | An open-ended question touching unsolved problems |

Age is only a guide. Follow curiosity, not the label.

## The MBTI Connection

Each thinker is tagged with their probable MBTI type — not to box them in, but to show that every cognitive style has contributed something irreplaceable to the sphere:

- **NT (Rational):** The architects of systems and theories — Newton, Einstein, Turing, Kant
- **NF (Idealist):** The visionaries and connectors — Plato, Tagore, Sagan, Vygotsky
- **ST (Guardian):** The careful builders of evidence — Darwin, Mendel, Curie, Linnaeus
- **SF (Artisan):** The observers of living reality — Faraday, Fleming, Goodall, Jenner

Parth uses MBTI to help students find thinkers who thought *like them* — and then to stretch into the unknown by encountering minds that think differently.

## The Unity Principle

Knowledge is not divided into school subjects. It is one sphere, constantly expanding.

- The borders between **physics and philosophy** are not walls — they are the most interesting places to stand (see: Bohr, Heisenberg, Kant)
- The borders between **mathematics and music** are not walls (see: Pythagoras, Euler, Leibniz)
- The borders between **biology and code** are not walls (see: Schrödinger, Turing, Vygotsky)

Every puzzle here points toward a connection — to another thinker, another sphere, another open question. Follow the connections. That is how the sphere grows.
